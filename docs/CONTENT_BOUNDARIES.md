# Content boundaries and references

## Ancient evidence and context

- Eleusis was a sanctuary and a place near Athens, where Demeter and Persephone were honored. See the official Hellenic Heritage site: https://www.hh.gr/en/destinations/eleusis/
- Ancient processions followed the Sacred Way from Athens to Eleusis, ending at the Telesterion. See the Acropolis Museum description: https://www.emfc.gr/en/temporary-exhibitions/eleusis-great-mysteries
- The pomegranate and Persephone's recurring time in the underworld are part of the myth tradition. See Metropolitan Museum: https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/248899
- The source dissertation on Crowley’s later *Rites of Eleusis* is Tracy W. Tupman (2003), *Theatre Magick: Aleister Crowley and the Rites of Eleusis*, Ohio State University.

## Modern creative design, not recovered ancient rites

- The five-beat myth and seven-movement interpretation are educational simplifications.
- The seven-step symbolic walking path is **not** a documented minute-by-minute order of initiation.
- The words/objects/actions framework is an interpretive performance grammar.
- The Return word, Seven Keys, and Chamber composer are new interactive exercises.
- Crowley’s Saturn-to-Luna cycle was produced in 1910, long after classical Eleusis.

Do not label historical claims as verified on the basis of passing UX or JavaScript tests alone.
